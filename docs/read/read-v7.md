# Read v7: a read code can rank, a reader can explain, and a record nobody can fudge (Oct 22 2026)

Built from the review (docs/read/review-oct22.md). Three things changed: the page has one owner per explanation and a
front door; the read is generated by a harness (code facts → reader → checks → page) instead of hand-written per
feeder; and rule records come from blind tests.

## The page

- **The front door (ReadQuestions).** A card above the Feeder File that answers the questions people follow an account
  for, each with the posts that prove it. See "The front door" below.
- **One owner per explanation.** The rule owns the why. A thing (node) owns what it is and what the account goes for
  with it, then code's numbers ("14 posts, 14 above its usual; best: …") and a link to the rule that decides it. The run
  owns what changed, as one line per rule event; only facts no rule owns get a card with a proof. The post mortem owns
  the post: what happens, and one clause from the rule it says most about (other rules are chips). The map's own post
  page is the post's place in the system and the way into its post mortem; it no longer repeats the post mortem.
  Ranks bands carry a code line on what each band is made of.
- **Records.** "Held 4 of 5 since run 1" counts only blind tests (below); a rule no post has tested yet says "New, not
  tested yet".
- **Takes by relationship.** A rule's "Do this" plate has four rows: if you're the account, up against it, working
  with it, and for your own posts.

## The front door: the questions (ReadQuestions, Oct 22, third version)

The first two front doors were rejected: a fact ticker with a paragraph ("amateur, no context"), then a run stage that
repeated the wall's runs. What the user wanted kept was the facts, with a stronger why, built on why someone follows an
account at all: its own team (what to post next, what's costing them, what grows them), a rival (their edge, what to
take, where they're exposed), a brand or agency (what partners get, whose crowd it is), someone learning (the move
behind the hits).

**The questions pass** (one Sonnet call after the last run; prompt `scripts/read_facts_prompt_v1.md`):

- Eight questions: grows, edge, costs, partners, crowd, turning (a series or habit sharpening or wearing out), steal,
  exposed. The reader answers the 5–8 the posts can prove, at most 2 a question, and skips the rest.
- Each answer: a claim of 9 words with the why inside it; proof as a contrast (the post that makes the point against
  one alike in everything but that) or a set; a tag per side (4 words) so a stranger reads the pair at a glance; the
  number that makes the contrast hit; a why of 55 words from the scroller's seat (the first seconds, who the account is
  being, what the viewer recognises); and what to do for each of the four readers, 18 words each.
- Every post gets its own why (30 words), which is now the post mortem's "Why it landed here".
- Pack: `read_v7_run.py answers <slug>` (the read so far with blind records, the follower line, code's 40 candidates,
  every post in full). Check: `read_v7_run.py check-answers <slug>`: numbers the pack printed, links, bare ids, banned
  words, lengths, tags, no copying a rule's why, and no post leading two answers (two answers on one post were one
  answer twice). One judge round from me was needed per feeder: it cut a costume answer that wasn't a cause, merged
  Lakmē's edge and steal (one claim), made Lakmē's follower answer honest, and added Anuj's "exposed".
- Cost: about one more run's worth (~₹4–6 per feeder each time the read updates).

**The component** (`ReadQuestions.tsx`, `readQuestions.css`; data `reader.qa` from `read_v7_to_page.py`):

- **The strip.** All 30 posts in order, each cover at the height it landed against the usual (log scale), the usual as
  a line, dates under it. An answer lifts its proof out of it (crimson ring on the post that makes the point, ink on the
  foil and the set) and threads run down from each lifted cover to its card.
- **The proof.** Hero vs foil as big covers with their tags, numbers ("218× his usual · 868K likes", "+1,796 followers
  that day") and labels; or a set of up to four (the strip lights the rest). Every cover opens its post mortem.
- **The answer.** The question, the claim, the number, the why with its post links, "For Anuj's team" (or a rival, a
  brand/partner, your own posts) and where to go: the post mortem, the rule it rests on, the next question.
- **Reading as.** Who's reading picks the "what to do" line and the order: a rival sees the edge and where the account
  is exposed first, a brand what partners get. Remembered per device; the index rows glide to their new places.
- **The index** (wide screens): every question with its claim, so the whole read scans at once; the active one fills as
  it holds. Phones and tablets get story segments instead, and a phone reads down: question, claim, the strip, the
  proof, the why.
- **Motion.** The usual line draws, the covers rise off it left to right, the rows come in; each answer's covers fly out
  of their places in the strip to their cards while the threads draw, the claim rises word by word, then the rest. Leaving,
  the covers fly home and the threads draw back. Transform and opacity only (the threads' draw excepted), at the app's
  tempo; it holds while you point at, touch or focus it, off screen and in a hidden tab; reduced motion = no motion, no
  tour. Every answer's text sits stacked in one cell, so changing answers never moves the page.
- **Lab** (no sign-in): `/_lab/readq/index.html?h=anuj.mp4` (`&dark=1`, `&w=390`, `&who=rival`, `&go=1,0` to drive the
  tour, `&still=1` for screenshots). Shots: desktop, tablet, phone, dark, each reader.

The Feeder File's reading lost its proof charts (they had no node on the map; each rule's page leads with its proof).

## The front door, final shape (ReadNow v4, Oct 22 night)

The questions card (above) was rejected for showing everything again (a strip, pulled-out posts, pairs: the wall and
the Feeder File already do that), and a first minimal version for having no structure. The front door now has the same
shape for every read:

- **The verdict.** One line on the account right now, and what it means.
- **Four tiles, always these, in this order:** Feeding (what's lifting it), Fading (what's dragging it), Odd (the
  anomaly a scroller never sees) and Next (the test the next posts will pass or fail). Each is one flat claim of 9 words
  at most and the one number behind it.
- **The tile in focus:** why, from the viewer's seat, and what to do for whoever is reading (team, rival, brand or
  partner, learner), with one way out to its post mortem or rule (a rule opens on the map).
- **Motion:** the verdict's words rise out of their line; the tiles come up in turn and their digits roll in; an ink
  lens glides to the focused tile and the tile turns over as it lands; the detail slides in from the side you're heading.
  The focus moves on by itself and holds on touch, hover or focus.
- **Reader:** `scripts/read_now_prompt_v2.md`, checked by `read_v7_run.py check-now` (four slots present, flat claims,
  no "vs", lengths, numbers the pack printed, one proof per tile). It also writes every live rule a 6–10 word title a
  stranger understands; the builder swaps it into each rule's `short`, so every surface (rule cards, chips, sheets,
  post mortems, the map's thing pages) names rules in full sentences.

## The Feeder File: a pick takes you there (Oct 22 night)

- Opening anything flies the camera to it: the system turns till it faces you and closes in to fit it and what it's
  tied to. Closing glides back to the view you had.
- Hover answers in about a sixth of a second (time-based easing, a 50ms map delay); nothing pops.
- What's open dominates: the open post's cover stays large however long the feed (a 100-post ring), lifted clear of its
  neighbours; everything else steps well back; only the open thing and its direct ties are named.
- Rule cards read the same way every time: "Rule 02 · Held 3 of 5 since run 2", the rule as a sentence, the side
  that lands (▲) and the side that sinks (▼), its number, its picture.
- Lab without sign-in: `/_lab/ff/index.html?h=anuj.mp4&open=P27`.

## The Read page, restructured (Oct 22, last)

The four-tile front door was rejected ("boxes with fancy animation won't change anything"), and the Feeder File and the
wall together were too much for one page. The page now:

- **The preview (ReadStories).** The read as stories, in the same order every time: the verdict (over a mosaic of all
  the posts), Feeding, Fading, Odd, Next, what the posts are made of (one thing per kind, how many of its posts
  landed), each rule as a sentence with its two sides and its record, then the door to the Feeder File. Each story is
  one post full bleed (no two stories share a post when another will do), one bold line, its number, why, what to do
  for whoever is reading, and its way to the proof. Instagram-style: bars, tap right for the next, left for the one
  before, hold to pause; each comes in over the last, its cover settling then drifting, its words rising, its digits
  rolling. Wide screens add the read's index beside it (each line a jump) and the Feeder File's door. Data:
  `reader.stories`, built by `read_v7_to_page.py` from the now (`now.json` v2), the rules and the map's things.
- **The wall** stays right below, by default.
- **The Feeder File is tucked away**: a full-screen layer over the read, opened from its door, a story's "On the
  map" (already flown to that post, rule or thing) or a rule or thing the wall names (`rd:law` / `rd:node`). On a
  phone it opens straight into the map's own full screen; closing that closes the layer. A run or a post mortem opened
  from the map hands back to the read below.

### The preview, as built now (ReadStories, Oct 22 late night)

- **What makes it a story is its top**: the bars (the one in view filling over the time it takes to read that story,
  6–16 s from its word count, then the next comes on by itself; the last stays), the head (the feeder's own picture
  and handle, and the date of the story's post, as Instagram's) and a pause. Which story it is (Feeding, Rule 01…) is

## The harness

`apps/worker/scripts/`:

| Step | File | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Facts | `read_v7_facts.py` | Every candidate fact code can find and rank on its own (below) |
| Pack | `read_v7_run.py pack <feeder> <run>` | State after the last run, code's numbers, the follower line, the obvious driver, the ranked candidates, the blind record, the posts (new ones in full, each with its closest earlier posts) |
| Blind sort | `read_v7_run.py blind <feeder> <run>` | The new posts with no numbers, for a pass that sorts them onto the rules' sides |
| Reader | `read_run_prompt_v5.md` | One run's state and words, in the contract below (Sonnet in production) |
| Check | `read_v7_run.py check` | Bounces once: links, numbers the pack never printed, bare ids, banned words, lengths, a drop repeating a fact or another drop, any text re-explaining a rule's why, a rule its own posts break, a rule that restates the obvious driver |
| Apply | `read_v7_run.py apply` | Code mends bare ids, marks a failing rule strained, keeps leftovers as notes for the next pack, scores the blind sort, writes the state |
| Page | `read_v7_to_page.py [--apply]` | The app's data (v6 files kept as `*.v6.json`) |
| Eval | `read_v7_eval.py` | Code alone against the hand-written read; the reader's uptake of code; repetition |

**What the reader writes** (`read_run_prompt_v5.md`): the account (who, a gist that names the obvious driver once),
lanes (for code to measure), 3–6 rules (law, sides, stat, why from the viewer's seat, a proof with timestamps for twins,
one line per post, four takes, limits, what this run did to it, the candidates it stands on), dropped rules in the
reader's own words, things (what, what the account goes for, the rule that decides it; never why), the run (head, take,
up to 3 "since" lines, facts no rule owns, the call settled, the next call, watch), each new post (label, what happens,
a note only when no rule explains it), 4–10 drops, open questions.

**The blind sort.** From run 2 on, before the reader sees anything, a separate pass gets only the rules and the new posts'
content, with no likes and no places, and puts each new post on a rule's side (or none). Code then checks where each
post landed. A post on a rule's winning side keeps it when it lands above the midpoint between the rule's two sides'
typical posts; beating a weak other side isn't enough. A rule's record on the page is only these tests, counted since its
words last changed. A rule failing blind (under half, 3+ tests) that the reader leaves alone is marked strained.

**Cost.** A run's pack is about 10,000 words at 30 posts (about 14k tokens) plus the 2,600-word prompt; the reader
writes about 4,000 words. On Sonnet 5.5 batch that's about ₹5 a run; the blind sort is under ₹1. At 100 posts (older
posts in short) a run is about ₹7. Three runs a month at 30 posts: about ₹20, plus the fingerprints.

## How much of "what matters" can code decide?

Code ranks every candidate by the size of the gap (likes over the format's usual, in log terms), the posts behind
each side, how consistently they sit on their side, and whether it's a lever the account controls. It flags what may be
something else: a gap that's mostly one format, or that vanishes without the obvious driver's posts.

- **Code alone finds most of the "what".** Against the hand-written read: Anuj 5 of 9 facts, Lakmē 7 of 8, with the
  strongest in its top 3 (the chant's follower day #1, the 48× acids-vs-ice twin #1–2).
- **It can't see meaning.** The same joke in other words (the chant against the drone sketch), a topic (football),
  a role (the extra in someone else's bit), "nothing to do" in a shelf post. Two things close part of that gap without
  embeddings: the reader's lanes, which code then measures, and each new post's closest earlier posts by rare shared
  words. That's how the 65× twin was found.
- **The reader leans on code.** Every final rule cites code candidates (Anuj 3 of 3, Lakmē 3 of 3), and every header
  drop is one of code's candidates, in code's order.
- **Code keeps the score.** Places, ratios, the follower line, deal marks (all nine of Anuj's deals found from Instagram's
  marks and the fingerprints), records (blind), and every number the reader quotes is checked against the pack.

So: code decides what deserves attention and keeps everyone honest; the reader decides what it means and says it.

## Results

Two passes over the same 30 posts per feeder, three runs of 10 each, the way production would run them, on Sonnet.

**Pass 1** (prompt v5, records counted on posts after a rule was written) proved the shape: the loop found what the
hand-written read found, and more ("in the big ones a friend repeats what the feed told her"; "a paid label doesn't
sink a post, a pitch up front does"). Its weakness: the reader placed new posts on its rules' sides after seeing their
numbers, so records like 9 of 9 were partly self-graded.

**Pass 2** (prompt v5.1, closest earlier posts, the obvious-driver check, the blind sort, strict midpoint scoring, a
third feeder):

- Most rules written from 10 posts failed their first blind round (Anuj 2 of 6, 2 of 8; Kendra 1 of 5), and the
  reader dropped them in its own voice: "My first guess. Then the Don YG stream clip sat on my 'needs the stream' side
  and took 3.7× his usual…"; "Sorted before the likes, it kept one of three… the face decided it, not the gift."
- What survived is what the hand-written read believed most, now tested:
  - Lakmē: **"Show the hack being done in the first seconds, then fix it"**, blind 5 of 5 (the chilli episode at 74×,
    the actives episode flopping at 1.9×).
  - Anuj: **"Both sides of a grievance the viewer already holds, played by him"**, with the drone sketch (sting at 0:18)
    against the chant (flip at 0:05) as its twin; **"A deal holds when the bit comes first and the product after"**
    (the Amazon game names the sale at 1:06; the ads that sank stop for the product at 0:28 and 0:44).
- **Kendra Scott** (a jewelry brand, from thinner older fingerprints): "A claim to settle or a moment to join beats a
  launch line" (a bride's wedding morning at 3.3×), "Each retelling of one event earns fewer likes than the first" (a
  pop-up at 3,201 likes, then 1,260), two first guesses dropped blind. The same prompt reads a creator, a beauty brand
  and a jewelry brand without changes.
- Against the hand-written read (judged by hand): Anuj covers 6 of its 9 facts (missing football, the collab premiere,
  the post after the chant), Lakmē 7 of 8 (the shelf is only partly there).

**Repetition** (same measures as the review, on the built page data):

| | Hand-written (v6) | Generated (v7) |
|---|---|---|
| Words on the page, Anuj / Lakmē | 4,904 / 4,707 | 3,839 / 3,757 |
| A rule's why retold elsewhere (4-word phrases) | 16% / 16% | 3% / 5% |
| …as whole clauses (6-word runs) | 6% / 7% | 0% / 1% |

What's left in common between places is pointers: a post's one-line reason on its rule page and its post mortem, a
run's change line in the rule's history.

## Not done yet

- **Fewer rules.** Blind testing kills weak rules, so after 30 posts each feeder has 3 live ones, not v6's 5. Some
  true v6 insights didn't come back (Anuj's football, the collab premiere). More runs (up to 100 posts) are where they
  should earn their place.
- **The questions are judged by me, not yet by a second model.** Both feeders needed one round of notes (repeated
  answers, one weak one, one missing). A Haiku judge with those notes as its checklist is the next step before this runs
  unattended. The old drops (`reader.drops`) still exist in the data but nothing shows them now.
- **The obvious-driver check over-fires** when a narrower rule lives inside the driver's lane (Lakmē's recap rule).
- **Not seen in the signed-in app.** The questions card was checked on its lab page (desktop, tablet, phone, dark, each
  reader, the tour driven); the rest of the
  page (rule pages with takes, records, runs, post mortems) was checked by building the data the engines read and a
  syntax check, not visually. Your dev server will show it.
- **Other kinds of account** (a team, a streaming brand, a mega brand) need fingerprints first, which are paid calls.
  The adapters from the review (page types, jobs, outside context) aren't built yet; Kendra Scott ran without them.
