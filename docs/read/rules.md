# Read: how the reader writes rules

Read has two layers: **What's on** (what the account is serving right now: campaigns, products, partners, deals, audience, moments; see pipeline.md) and **the rules** (why it lands). This file covers the rules.

A rule is a mechanism inside the account's posts that decides where a post lands, proven by its own posts. Read shows it in the rule rows exactly as they are designed (the big "places" number, the kicker, the law, the with/without line, and when opened, the why, the two decks and the folds). Only the words change.

## The depth bar

A rule fails if a casual follower of the account already knows it. "He works when he roasts people" fails. Every rule carries three layers:

1. **What literally happens**, step by step, from the postcards: timestamps, who is in frame, who says what, where the brand sits. "His best sketches pay off between 0:03 and 0:16. His street bits make you wait till 0:55 and stall at 14th to 20th."
2. **Why his crowd responds**, read the way someone who lives in the feed would read it. "Andheri East" and "UP" are the same pun with the same timing; only Andheri East feels at home in his feed, because it's his snobbery aimed at a suburb his own crowd lives with. Mechanics can't see that. Living the feed can.
3. **The take**: one plain sentence a creator, brand or agency can act on. "Land the first laugh inside ten seconds, never at the end."

Character goes in the header line ("A man far too sure of himself…"), not in the rules.

## What the reader reads

- **Only postcards** (POST NAME / WORK / FLOW with timestamps / TEXT & REFERENCES / MECHANIC FINGERPRINT), plus the date and the rank code stamps on each. Never raw fingerprints.
- **Every format together.** Ranks stay per format (reels against reels, carousels against carousels), but the reader reads everything the account serves at once, so a rule can cite a carousel next to a reel. Anuj is all reels; Lakmé (half carousels) is the first real test.
- **Postcard gap found:** when one creator plays several characters (wigs, filters, split profiles, an off-camera "caller"), the postcard often doesn't say it's the same person; the fingerprint does. Postcards need to record it, or the reader can't see the every-part-himself rule.

## How a run works

Every run of 10 posts, the reader gets:

1. **The fresh 10 postcards**, with ranks.
2. **What it concluded last run**: the live rules, the dropped ones, the open questions, and the call it made.
3. **The whole window so far**: every earlier postcard and rank, so a pattern in the fresh 10 can be checked against everything.

It then births, sharpens, narrows or drops rules; settles last run's call; and makes a new one. Feed Me isn't a full account history (three months at most); it says what the account is doing right now, as clearly as possible.

## Writing rules

- **Law:** 8 words or fewer, plain words.
- **Why:** literal receipts first (posts named by what happens in them, with timestamps and ranks: "the Ludo board at 0:08 (7th)"), then the crowd's reason, then the take.
- **Lands / sinks:** every post the law speaks for. A post that breaks the law stays listed and gets a one-line reason. A rule with no breaks is suspicious.
- **History:** one evidence line per run. The kicker already says new, stronger or narrowed, so never write "Hunch:" or "Became a rule:".
- **Check before shipping:** every timestamp, rank and "who does what" must be on the postcard. An independent check of the first Anuj draft killed one rule ("the last line has to be his": four top-10 posts don't end on his line) and fixed six timestamps and descriptions.
- A rule needs two or more posts to be born. One clean hit with no miss goes under **Still open**.
- Drop a rule the moment the evidence says it was something else, and say what it folded into.
- Two honest rules beat four padded ones.
- Every account gets a **brand deals** rule after three or more deals. It's the rule people compare across accounts most.

## Lakmé, replayed run by run (reels from postcards, carousels and images from captions)

89 posts from Apr 28 to Jun 14, in nine runs, each ranked against its own format (48 reels, 34 carousels, 7 images). Its carousels and images were never fingerprinted and their media links have expired, so the reader only had their captions; every rule that leans on one says so.

| # | Rule | Right | Take |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | It needs a face they know, or a real creator. | 22 of 23 | Put Kareena, Aneet or a real creator on it; brand copy in a model's mouth is wallpaper. |
| 02 | Its big numbers come from co-posts. | 6 of 10 | Co-post every partner reel, with partners whose crowd cares about beauty. |
| 03 | Dressing it up as a screen or a graphic kills it. | 22 of 23 | Show the product plainly, on skin if you can; never wrap it in a fake interface. |

- The co-post rule's anchor: the same Green Matcha CC+ got 831 on Lakmé's own launch and 157,100 the next day as a creator's co-post. Its counterexample: a co-post with a Bengali web series got 185.
- The face rule's trap: models speaking brand copy in the first person sink ("My one-stop fix", 39th). The exception: anonymous women in the crowd's own meme format ("Me at Lakmé", 13th).
- Dropped in run 7: "Lip gloss is its hit product". Its own gloss posts got 350 and 167; the 309,865 was a co-post.
- Still open: what on the carousels made the best ones work, and whether the creator's SPF blush reel (3,542, no tag) was co-posted.
- An independent check of the first Lakmé draft narrowed two rules: "on skin" became "no screen gimmick" (five on-skin reels landed outside the middle; plain product reels did fine), and "a voice like theirs" became "a real creator".

## First pass on Traya and Srishti (below the bar)

These were drafted before the depth bar above, from 90 days of captions and day-7 likes. They read the account's character, not the mechanics inside its posts, so they need redoing from postcards once those feeders have postcards again. They were drafted from captions and day-7 likes (ranked by likes within the same media type, because stored percentiles are broken for these feeders, see the end). None of the posts were watched: none of these feeders has had a post fingerprinted since June. Every rule is a first read, so every kicker would say "New".

### @traya.health · hair-loss brand · 739k followers · usual own post ≈ 88 likes

**Who:** A 739k-follower hair clinic that's ignored when it talks, and huge when a comedian does.
**Formula:** Someone else's voice, his own words, the answer up front.

**01 · Traya wins when it stays out of the joke.** (brand deals)
- Landed: a creator's "damadon ki izzat" family skit, Traya only in the hashtags (30,712). Missed: a skit from a creator of the same size that ends "aur baalon ka comeback? Traya pe chhod do" (384).
- Line: Two paid family skits from creators of the same size. The one that never mentions hair got 30.7k likes. The one that ends on a Traya line got 384.
- Why: The moment the joke turns into a hair-loss pitch, the crowd smells the ad. Skits that keep Traya to a hashtag ride the creator's reach untouched. Four of them hold 98% of the likes across Traya's ranked posts.
- Where it breaks: None of it sticks. The day of the 127k-like skit added fewer followers than an average day.

**02 · Give the answer, not the appointment.**
- Landed: "More hair shedding after starting minoxidil? Don't panic, it can happen" (146). Sank: "Can you step out in the sun after minoxidil? Get the answer from our doctor" (70), the same week.
- Line: Two minoxidil reels in one week. "Don't panic, it can happen" landed. "Get the answer from our doctor" sank.
- Why: His crowd is anxious and scrolling. A post that answers the fear in its first line gets kept. One that sends him to the doctor or the video gets skipped. All 7 "our doctor explains" reels sit in the bottom half.
- Where it breaks: It only lifts the floor. The best answered reel still got 146 likes.

**03 · Ask his question in his words.**
- Landed: a card asking "Sugar se baal jhadte hain?" (545). Sank: "More Minoxidil is not Faster Hair Growth 🚫" (128), two days later.
- Line: "Sugar se baal jhadte hain?" in his own Hindi landed. A minoxidil rule in clinic English sank.
- Why: He worries about his hair in Hindi. A card that asks his question back starts a conversation; a rule in English tells him he's doing it wrong.
- Where it breaks: Not tried since July. Every card since sits at 65 to 156.

**Still open:** Did the hair-pull-test reel (2,174) win because you can do the test along with it, or because it was boosted? It had 1,612 likes on day one. And will the October "no false promises" run earn trust that likes can't show? It has the fewest comments of any stretch.

### @srishtigargg · lifestyle creator · 444k followers (down 2% since July) · usual post ≈ 7k likes

**Who:** The internet's big sister with one brain cell left. Her crowd comes for her and leaves when a brand does the talking.
**Formula:** Her voice, her people, the product inside the joke.

**01 · A product only works inside her joke.** (brand deals)
- Landed: "my boyfriend, the human embodiment of 'we have time', got me the Maybelline cushion foundation" (5,672). Sank: a Kérastase padel event in brand copy, "Gloss On. Game On." (900, her worst post in 90 days).
- Line: Maybelline as a boyfriend joke held her usual numbers. Kérastase's padel event in brand copy was her worst post.
- Why: Her crowd follows a voice: lowercase, self-roasting, boy trouble. A deal that keeps the voice passes; one that makes her a host or a billboard (Visa, KitKat, Sephora) gets scrolled. Her own label follows the same rule: the Sundazed top under "we're too sexy for all this stress" got 13,610; "our limited edition collection" got 1,892.
- Where it breaks: Even a good deal only matches an average post. All 10 brand posts sit below her median.

**02 · Tell the day, not the thank-you.**
- Landed: her first Ganpati at home, told as friends, family and modaks (20,370). Sank: the next day's post about the same Ganpati, thanking the decorator (6,549).
- Line: Her first Ganpati at home, told through her people, landed. The next day's thank-you to the decorator sank.
- Why: Her big days work when they're about who was there. Turn the same day into a credit list and it reads like work.
- Where it breaks: The pair is also carousel vs reel, so format may be part of it. The Elle cover, a team-credit caption, still did fine at 7,347.

**Still open:** Captions don't separate her best reels from her worst: emoji-only captions sit at both 35.7k and 2k. The difference is in the clip itself, which we can't see until her posts are fingerprinted again.

## Data problems found while drafting

- **Follower counts are broken, not just odd.** Lakmé: 80 of 118 day-7 rows have the wrong count (41 are a 3,000,000 placeholder, 34 sit at 397k–420k, 3 are under 110k; the real count is ~2.77M). Traya: 17 rows have a count in the hundreds. Srishti: one post at ~86k. Stored engagement rate and percentiles can't be trusted for these feeders.
- **On collab posts, the stored follower count looks like the creator's**, not the feeder's (Traya collabs show 544k–1.14M against Traya's ~700k).
- **No fingerprints since June** for any of the three: every window post has no fingerprint, condensation or breakdown, and Srishti's media assets are all `pending_capture`.
- **Gaps in checkpoints:** Aug 14–20 has no metrics at all on Srishti and Traya; Sep 26–30 has only d1/d3 on all three.
- **Fields that are always empty:** `collab_post` and `duration_seconds` are null, and `carousel_slide_count` is 1 on every carousel.
