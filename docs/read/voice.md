# How the Read sounds (Oct 27 2026)

The user's verdict on the v12 copy: "It makes sense but lacks the finesse of how a smart human would articulate their
thoughts." The facts are right; the sentences are assembled. This file is the voice sample every Read writer and editor
matches. The editing pass also applies `apps/worker/scripts/read_humanizer.md` (blader/humanizer, MIT), which catalogues
the general AI tells; the notes below are the ones specific to us.

## What makes our copy read as machine-made

1. **Every sentence is a stat plus an example.** "All 14 of her posts beat its usual, typically 4.1×, and they hold 90% of
   the likes of all 30 posts." A person says what it means first, then gives the one or two numbers that prove it.
2. **Posts are re-described in full every time.** "Aneet stopping a woman rubbing chilli on her lips" appears five times
   on one page. Name a post fully once, then use a short handle a friend would use: "the chilli reel".
3. **Number chains.** "at 92× its usual and ... at 74× its usual and ... at 65× its usual". Two numbers a sentence at most;
   say "his usual" once and let later numbers stand alone.
4. **The same skeleton everywhere.** "N of M beat its usual, typically X×" opens every node. Vary it; open on the point.
5. **Display names as nouns.** "Stream clips, Photo posts and Plays a type mostly sink." Write it the way you'd say it:
   "his stream clips, photo dumps and character bits mostly sink".
6. **No read.** A paragraph that lists what happened without saying what it means for the account is a table in prose.
   Every paragraph has one point, said plainly, with an opinion where the numbers earn one.

## Rules of thumb

- Lead with the read, then prove it. One point a paragraph.
- Two numbers a sentence at most. "His usual" once a sentence.
- Name a post once in full, then by its handle. Link text can be the handle.
- Plain verbs, short words, varied sentence length. A little attitude is fine when the numbers earn it ("earns him almost
  nothing"); no slang, no exclamation marks, no em dashes.
- Keep every fact and number from the draft, and add none. If a sentence needs a number the pack doesn't have, write the
  sentence without it.
